Passkey device check

Sealed data can use a passkey or security key as one factor: the device returns a secret that cannot be guessed (the WebAuthn PRF extension). Whether that works depends on your browser and your device. This page tests it here, in your browser. Nothing is sent anywhere and nothing is stored; the passkey you create stays on your device.

1. This device

2. Create a test passkey

Your device asks you to confirm (fingerprint, face, PIN, or a touch on the key). This makes a test passkey for this website only; you can delete it afterwards in your device's passkey settings.

3. Test it

Asks the passkey for its secret three times (twice with the same input, once with another) and runs a small vault test: a vault opened by this passkey plus a second factor, and refused with the passkey alone. You will be asked to confirm each time your device wants it.

4. The result, to copy

This block holds only technical facts (browser, support, yes/no results, a short fingerprint). It contains no key and nothing that identifies you. Send it to the project to say what works on your device.

The fingerprint is the first 4 bytes of a SHA-256 of a value derived from the passkey's secret: the same passkey and input give the same fingerprint every time, on every device it syncs to, if the platform returns the same secret there. Why this matters
